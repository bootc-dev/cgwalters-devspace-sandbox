## r4495701189 (`ostree-sysroot-cleanup.c:195`, rm_rf when boot_fd is -1)

Right, that guard was wrong. Cleanup now calls `_ostree_sysroot_ensure_boot_fd()`
first and always does the rm_rf, and the initramfs-overlays cleanup no longer
special-cases -1 either. I folded that commit into the one that opens boot_fd
from /sysroot/boot, since it only exists for that one.

## r4495728648 (`ot-admin-builtin-finalize-staged.c:74`, holding /boot open)

Agreed, an ESP at /boot isn't ours to hold. `--hold` no longer opens /boot
unconditionally: as before, it relies on the sysroot's boot_fd, and it only also
holds /boot when /boot is the bind mount of /sysroot/boot (same st_dev/st_ino).
That is the case in this bug: gpt-auto's `boot.automount` says "EFI System
Partition", but what it triggers is ostree's own bind `boot.mount`. If that
expires while a deployment is staged, stopping `boot.mount` stops
ostree-finalize-staged.service (RequiresMountsFor=/boot) and finalizes early.
A real ESP at /boot is left to expire.

## r4496005210 (`ostree-sysroot.c:539`, `const`)

`booted_boot_path()` returns one of two string literals, so `const char *` is
right and there's nothing to free. I said so in its comment. It's no longer
used in `_ostree_sysroot_ensure_writable()` anyway.

## r4496033505 (`ostree-sysroot.c:547`, the grub2-mkconfig fallback)

Agreed, it's not needed. `_ostree_sysroot_ensure_writable()` now remounts
/sysroot, and remounts /boot only when /boot is its own filesystem. When /boot
is on the physical root, boot_fd is /sysroot/boot, which the /sysroot remount
already makes writable, so nothing at /boot is touched. That drops the clone,
the remount-failure fallback and the log-once flag. Path-based backends like
grub2-mkconfig on a separate /boot behave exactly as before.
